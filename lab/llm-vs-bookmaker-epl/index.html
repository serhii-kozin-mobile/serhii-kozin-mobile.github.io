Home / Lab / Local 3B LLM vs bookmaker odds, 380 Premier League matches

Lab study · Oct 26, 2026

I gave a small local LLM the bookmaker's odds. It made them worse.

ReviewedDone, independently reviewed. Full write-up coming in November.

0.2053 → 0.2190

Ranked probability score of the pre-closing odds before and after the model "adjusted" them (lower is better). Given the market's odds, it made them worse.

The question

How close does a free local 3B model get to bookmaker closing odds on every 2025-26 Premier League match, and does handing it the odds help? This is a forecasting backtest on historical data: no bets, no betting account, no tips.

Setup

  • All 380 matches of the 2025-26 Premier League. Results and odds from football-data.co.uk; baselines from 2021-22 to 2024-25.
  • Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (Q4_K_M) on CPU through llama.cpp, JSON-constrained home/draw/away output. Median 5.8 s per match, $0 API cost.
  • No look-ahead: every prompt is built from results before that match. The model was released before the season started.
  • Baselines: closing and pre-closing market odds, a simple Elo, historical base rates. Paired per-match gaps with 4,000-sample bootstrap CIs.

What came out

  • Nobody beat the closing odds. RPS: closing odds 0.2045, Elo 0.2095, LLM with odds 0.2190, LLM alone 0.2255, base rates 0.2278.
  • The LLM alone is no better than predicting the historical average. Paired gap vs base rates −0.0022 (95% CI −0.0140 to +0.0101). A short Elo script beats it by 0.0160 RPS.
  • It ignores home advantage. Average forecast for home wins 30.5%; actual 42.6%. It made the draw its outright favourite in 22.1% of matches. The market never did.
  • It answers from a template. 47 distinct probability triples across 380 matches; 35/30/35 alone appears 89 times.
  • A paper backtest (not advice) showed nothing: every ROI confidence interval crosses zero.

What this does not prove

  • One small 3B model at Q4 on a CPU. This measures the free local tier, not "AI" in general.
  • One season, one league: confidence intervals are wide, so read the paired gaps rather than the raw scores.
  • A single prompt format. Wording almost certainly moves the results.
